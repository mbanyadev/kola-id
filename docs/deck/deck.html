BEAC 2024

  • The same customer passes KYC again at every agent network, exchange and lending app. Each check costs about US$1 to 1.35 (Sumsub, Blockpass list prices).
  • Each app then stores ID scans it must protect under Cameroon's new data-protection law (No. 2024/017).
  • Outside finance it's worse: betting sites must keep under-21s out, buyers can't verify co-op members, employers can't verify diplomas.

The solution

Check once with a trusted issuer. Prove to any app, privately.

Issuer

Kumba Verify checks Manka once

Signs a credential. Only a commitment goes on-chain, into a registry of approved issuers.

→
Holder

Manka's wallet keeps it private

Her birth date, country and screening stay on her device as Midnight private state.

→
App

Sawa Exchange gets a yes

A zero-knowledge proof that she meets its public policy, plus a per-app nullifier.

One account per person: the nullifier stops duplicate sign-ups.

Unlinkable: a different nullifier for every app, so apps can't pool data to track her.

Revocable: the issuer can revoke; the proof then fails everywhere.

What Midnight makes possible

Public where it must be, private everywhere else

Public on Midnight

  • Approved issuer key hashes (who may issue)
  • Credential commitments in a Merkle tree, and revocations
  • Each app's policy: minimum age, allowed countries, screening freshness, epoch
  • Per-app nullifiers and a verification counter (for billing)

Never leaves the holder

  • Name, birth date, country, document
  • Which issuer checked her
  • Which other apps she uses
  • Her holder secret

In Compact, only what passes through disclose() becomes public: here the app id, the yes/no eligibility result, the nullifier, Merkle roots, the policy and the date. Never the credential's contents. Right: the demo's public ledger after the full story. Threat model: docs/THREAT_MODEL.md.

A co-op union can't mint ID checks and an institute can't mint memberships, because each registry approves only its own issuers. This is enforced by the existing contract and covered by tests. A typed claim field (one registry, many credential types) is on the v2 roadmap.

Business model (proposed)

Apps pay per proof. Issuers earn when their checks are reused.

Pilot
Free

first 1,000 proofs per app, for design partners

Standard
150 FCFA

(≈ €0.23) per sign-up proof, 10 FCFA per returning login. No monthly minimum.

Compliance · roadmap
300 FCFA

per proof, with issuer file access under a reliance agreement

  • 40% to the issuer whose credential was used; 60% to Kola ID. Billing is auditable from the on-chain verification counter.
  • Versus a fresh check: US$1.35 per verification on Sumsub Basic (US$149/month minimum) and US$1.00 to 1.35 on Blockpass (US$99/month minimum).
  • Regulated finance: CEMAC Regulation 02/24 (art. 69 to 72) lets institutions rely on a regulated third party for due diligence, with documents provided on request. Kola ID is the rails for that reliance, not a way around it.

Go-to-market and competition

Start with one issuer and one app per vertical

  1. KYC: a KYC provider or microfinance institution in Douala or Buea, with one exchange or lending app
  2. Co-ops: one South-West cocoa union, with one buyer preparing for EUDR
  3. Skills: one technical institute, with one job platform
  4. A hosted check page and webhook, so apps never touch Midnight directly
  5. Then mainnet, and the other five CEMAC countries, which share one AML regulation

Proposed plan. No issuers or apps have signed yet.
